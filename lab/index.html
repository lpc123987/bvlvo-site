Model assumptions, rules and metrics

Each buyer demands one unit per round. Suppliers have fixed prices and capacities. Buyers have an initial budget and maximum acceptable price; purchases reduce their remaining budget. Unfilled demand does not carry forward. Supply capacity resets each round; budgets do not. There is no negotiation, learning, logistics, real payment or model call.

  • A Price priority: process buyers by descending maximum acceptable price, then choose the cheapest available supplier. Trades use supplier prices; this is not an auction.
  • B Balanced service: prioritize buyers with the fewest cumulative purchases, break ties with round-based rotation, and choose the cheapest available supplier.
  • C Dispersed purchasing: use the same buyer order as B, but prioritize available suppliers with the fewest cumulative sales, then the lowest price. This may increase spending and does not guarantee better resilience.
  • Fill rate: units traded / 140. Spending uses simulation accounting units with no cash value. Buyer gap: the highest cumulative buyer purchases minus the lowest.
  • Supply HHI: sum of squared supplier sales shares; higher means more concentrated. Rounds 4–7 fill rate: units traded in those four rounds / 80. These metrics describe this model, not real-world predictions.
